On February 28, the United States and Israel launched strikes targeting Iran's missile infrastructure, killing longtime Iranian Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and other senior officials. According to President Donald Trump, the purpose of the strikes was to ensure that Iran does not obtain a nuclear weapon.

By March 2, approximately 555 people had died in Iran, according to the Iranian Red Crescent. More than 150 of those deaths resulted from a strike on a girls' school located near a military base. In addition, six U.S. service members have now died in operations against Iran.

In response to the attacks, Iran launched missiles and drones across the Middle East, targeting nations with U.S. military bases and other U.S.-connected interests.

The United States is currently engaged in military conflict, but it has not officially declared war. Similar to the situation in Venezuela earlier this year, President Trump ordered strikes against Iran using the broad presidential authority to engage in military action. However, the president does not have the constitutional authority to declare war without congressional approval. As a result, while the United States has entered a war-like state, there has been no formal declaration of war.

The United States' actions in Iran carry significant consequences that cannot be overlooked. The strikes risk escalating into a broader conflict in the Middle East, which could result in widespread death, violence, economic disruption, and geopolitical instability. President Trump's actions also set a new precedent for U.S.-Iranian relations by moving away from diplomacy. As a global superpower, the United States helps shape international norms, and other nations often look to it to set standards for what is considered acceptable conduct, particularly in international relations.
