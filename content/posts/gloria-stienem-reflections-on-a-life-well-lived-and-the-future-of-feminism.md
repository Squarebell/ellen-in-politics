Gloria Steinem died one week ago, on September 2, 2026, at the age of 92, after spending a lifetime fighting for women’s rights and serving as a leading figure in the second-wave feminist movement.

As an activist and journalist, her work contributed greatly to the passage of legislation advancing gender equality. Her voice also helped bring gender-related issues that were previously not talked about to the forefront of public discourse. In many ways, all women today have something to thank Gloria Steinem for. She helped to change how women were perceived- by the law, by society, and by themselves. 

2nd wave feminism, which emerged in the 1960s, fought for legislative equality in all aspects of a woman’s life, from family and marriage, to finances, employment, and education. This new 2nd wave followed the original women's rights movement, also known as 1st wave feminism, that centered around achieving a woman's right to vote. In between these first two periods of feminism, there was a dull phase, spanning roughly from the 1920s to the 1950s, in which feminism still existed but as a movement was much less unified and visible.  But just because women had gained the right to vote didn't mean there was no more progress to be made. Gloria Steinem was one of the key figures who recognized that the fight for women's rights was far from over. She played a major role in reigniting the feminist cause in this 2nd wave. 

Gloria Steinem demonstrated that progress within feminism does not signal the end of the movement, but rather its continued evolution in response to the challenges that remain. Following the second wave of feminism, feminism continued to evolve, shifting its focus toward issues such as sexual harassment and giving rise to the #MeToo movement. Looking toward the future, we should view the progress achieved through Gloria Steinem’s advocacy, alongside the work of countless others, as steppingstones toward greater equality- not as a reason to throw in the towel. As Ms. Steinem said herself, "People now ask me if I'm passing the torch. I always explain that no, I'm keeping my torch, thank you very much. And I'm using it to light the torches of others."

Feminism may look different today than it did in the 1960s, but it remains both necessary and relevant in our society. Because men and women in the United States are now largely equal under the law- with the major exception of reproductive rights- there has been a growing perception that feminism has aged out of relevance and is no longer necessary. This could not be further from the truth. Women remain underrepresented in positions of power, lack full reproductive rights, are disproportionately affected by crime and violence, and are underrepresented in medical research. The list goes on. Patriarchal ideas continue to be embedded within our society, and women are consistently held back by double standards and misogynist ideology. To honor the life of Gloria Steinem, the most powerful thing we can do is continue the fight for gender equality.
